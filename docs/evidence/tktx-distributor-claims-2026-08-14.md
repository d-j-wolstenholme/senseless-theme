# Evidence — TKTX "only official UK distributor" claims

**Captured 2026-08-14.** Substantiation for the `provenance` band and FAQ q3 on
`templates/page.tktx-numbing-cream-uk.json`. Retained because that page makes a statement of fact
about third parties, and a statement of fact about a third party needs to survive being
challenged. Re-capture before the page is materially edited.

## What the page claims

> "On 14 August 2026 we opened two of those websites. Each described itself as the *only*
> certified or official distributor, and neither published a company number, a VAT number or an
> ingredients list on the page we read."

and, in the FAQ:

> "When we checked in August 2026, more than one UK site described itself as the only certified
> or official distributor."

Both are claims about **two** named sources, checked on one date. That is the whole of the
substantiation and the copy is written not to exceed it.

## The captures

### 1. `https://www.tk-tx.com/` — fetched 2026-08-14

Verbatim, as returned:

- "The Only Authentic, Trusted Supplier"
- "The only certified and trusted stockist for 100% authentic TKTX Numbing Cream"
- "At TKTX Official, you can rest assured we only supply genuine TKTX numbing cream"
- "we are proud to be the only certified distributor of TKTX numbing cream"

Absent from the page read: UK registered company name, company number, VAT number, INCI /
ingredients list. Footer carries a copyright line ("Copyright © 2017 - 2026") and policy links.

### 2. `https://www.tktxtattoos.com/` — fetched 2026-08-14

Verbatim, as returned:

- "The only certified and trusted stockist for 100% authentic TKTX Numbing Cream"
- "Here at TKTX Official we are proud to be the only certified distributor of TKTX numbing cream."

Absent from the page read: UK registered company name, company number, VAT number, INCI /
ingredients list.

## What was NOT verified, and was therefore removed from the page

An earlier draft said *"several of them carry their own guidance on how to identify counterfeit
stock, which tells you something about the market they are operating in."*

**Removed 2026-08-14.** Two problems with it:

1. **"Several" (≥3) was supported by n=2.** Other such sites appeared in search results
   (`tktxoriginaluk.co.uk`, `tktx-usa.com`, `skinartdepot.co.uk`) but were never fetched, so the
   only first-hand evidence was the two above. A claim sourced to a search-results snippet is not
   a claim we can stand behind about a named competitor.
2. **The trailing clause was innuendo attached to an unevidenced fact.** "Which tells you
   something about the market they are operating in" invites an inference we had not established
   and did not need. Under the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 the
   denigration limb is the one a competitor comparison actually trips on, and that sentence was
   where this page was closest to it.

## Still open — not a compliance question

"TKTX" appears in the page slug, `<title>`, H1 and breadcrumb of a commercial page selling a
competing product. That is a **trade-mark** question (Trade Marks Act 1994 s.10(3), and BPMMR
reg 4(g) on unfair advantage), not an MHRA or ASA one, and it is outside what this project can
clear. **Route to MHG legal.** Nothing in the copy makes a misleading comparison — the page
compares nothing on performance, price or safety — but the use of the mark itself needs a view.
